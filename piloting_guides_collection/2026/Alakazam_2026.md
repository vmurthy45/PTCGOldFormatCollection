# How to Pilot — Alakazam (2026)

_Format: Scarlet & Violet-on Standard, the Mega-era Dragapult format. This is the
single-prize control/combo build that **Cerys Jones piloted to the Indianapolis Regional
Championship**, going an absurd **7–0 against Dragapult** on the day — turning what had
always been Alakazam's worst matchup into its best._

## One-line identity
Draw your whole deck, then hit for everything at once. **Alakazam's Powerful Hand** places
**2 damage counters (20 damage) for every card in your hand**, so a hand of 12–14 one-shots
*anything* in the format for a single Energy. It's a **one-prize deck**, so it preys on the
linear two-prize decks that can't profitably multi-prize your little Psychic guys — and this
particular build bolts on a Dragapult-hate package that flips the format's boogeyman on its
head.

## Why it's a meta call
One-prize decks (Lucario, Raging Bolt, Hydreigon) already beat up the linear two-prize field
because those decks hate trading two prizes for a single-prize Pokémon. Alakazam's one
historic hole was **Dragapult** — Phantom Dive spreads damage and can knock out **two Abra/
Kadabra pieces at once**, which a single-prize deck simply can't keep pace with on resources
or prizes. Cerys's insight was simple: *if the deck is already good into the rest of the
field, fixing the one bad matchup makes it a great call.* Alakazam is uniquely suited to this
because it has **the best draw engine of any one-prize deck and a pile of free card slots** —
it doesn't need a specific Stadium, Supporter, or Tool for its attack, so you can pour those
slots into tech.

## The engine (how the damage happens)
- **Alakazam (56/132) ×3** — 140 HP. **Powerful Hand** `[P]`: place **2 damage counters on
  the opponent's Active for each card in your hand**. Ability **Psychic Draw**: when you play
  it from hand to evolve, **draw 3**. So the attack and the draw are the same card.
- **Kadabra (55/132) ×4 / Abra (54/132) ×4** — **Psychic Draw** on Kadabra draws **2** as you
  evolve. You can play **multiple** Kadabra/Alakazam from hand in a single turn (evolving
  different Abras) to re-trigger the draws over and over — this is why the deck can "do all of
  them multiple times a turn."
- **Dunsparce (120/159) ×3 → Dudunsparce (129/162) ×3** — **Run Away Draw**: draw **3**, then
  **shuffle Dudunsparce and everything on it back into your deck**. It's a *reusable* +3 that
  also thins your deck — replay Dunsparce and do it again next turn.
- The math: 20 × (cards in hand). Ten cards = 200, twelve = 240, fourteen = 280. You draw the
  house first, *then* swing. **Rare Candy (125/132) ×3** lets you jump Abra → Alakazam for a
  fast draw-3 KO when you can't afford the Kadabra step (you trade Kadabra's +2 for speed).

## The anti-Dragapult package (the innovation)
Older Alakazam lists tried to survive Dragapult by **protecting the bench** (Battle Sage,
Forest Seal Stone, Mew/Shaymin bench-barriers). Cerys scrapped all of it and instead went for
**stopping the attack, not the spread**:
- **Elgyem (40/86) ×1** — **Slight Shift** `[P]`: **move an Energy from one of the opponent's
  Pokémon to another of their Pokémon.** Against Dragapult you relocate the Energy they just
  manually attached onto a useless benched Pokémon, so their attacker comes up short. Deny the
  early manual attachments and **they're forced to spend a Supporter on Crispin every single
  turn just to have Energy** — which means they *aren't* drawing with Lillie. That's the whole
  lock: make them find too many pieces for the privilege of doing nothing.
- **Nighttime Mine (197/217) ×4** — a Stadium (its printed text taxes **Tera** attackers `[C]`
  more, which is real value into **Wellspring Ogerpon** and friends). Against Dragapult its job
  is the **stadium war**: four copies bury the opponent's own Stadium (Team Rocket's Watchtower
  / Jamming Tower / Area Zero) so it never sticks. Dragapult increasingly *needs* that Stadium;
  deny it and the deck chokes.
- **Genesect (40/64) ×1** — **ACE Nullifier**: while it has a **Tool attached, the opponent
  can't play ACE SPEC cards.** This shuts off Dragapult's **Unfair Stamp** (their key
  disruption), plus Prime Catcher, Hero's Cape, Maximum Belt, and Secret Box across the field.
  Because Alakazam doesn't care *which* Tools it runs, you get this for free.

## Support / tech (why the odd 1-ofs)
- **Enhanced Hammer (148/167) ×2 + Dedenne (87/191) ×1** — Enhanced Hammer discards an
  opponent's **Special Energy**; **Dedenne's Electromagnetic Sonar** puts a Trainer back from
  the discard, so you **loop the Hammers** and strip Special Energy every turn. This is the
  Crustle/Malamar answer ("once they run out of Energy they just get clobbered by Alakazam")
  and it bites Cynthia's Garchomp's **Rocky Fighting Energy** too.
- **Handheld Fan (150/167) ×1** — Tool: when its holder is Active and gets hit, **move an
  Energy from the attacker to the opponent's bench.** Swings **Festival Lead / Miss-Energy /
  Lopunny / Garchomp** positive — they attack, you bump their Energy back and they can't follow
  up.
- **Lucky Helmet (158/167) ×1** — Tool: **draw 2 when your Active is damaged.** Judge
  insurance — if they Judge you to a small hand and then attack, you refill.
- **Dawn (87/94) ×4 / Hilda (84/86) ×3** — the search Supporters: **Dawn** grabs a Basic +
  Stage 1 + Stage 2 (the whole Abra line at once); **Hilda** grabs an Evolution + an Energy.
- **Poké Pad (81/88) ×4 / Buddy-Buddy Poffin (144/162) ×4** — raw draw and board-building
  (Poffin fetches two ≤70-HP Basics: Abra, Dunsparce, Elgyem, Dedenne).
- **Sacred Ash (168/182) ×1 / Lana's Aid (155/167) ×1 / Night Stretcher (196/217) ×1** — the
  recovery suite. Shuffle up to 5 Pokémon back (Sacred Ash), or pull KO'd non-Rule-Box Pokémon
  and Basic Energy back to hand (Lana's Aid) — vital against Zoroark's hand-discard and general
  attrition.
- **Boss's Orders (114/132) ×2** — gust to pick the KO or drag up the Pokémon you've stranded
  with Elgyem.
- **Shaymin (10/182) ×1** — **Flower Curtain** prevents damage to your benched non-Rule-Box
  Pokémon. The Nighttime Mine/Elgyem lock is *why* many builds can cut Shaymin entirely; this
  list keeps one as extra insurance for the Abra/Kadabra bench versus Phantom Dive.
- **Fezandipiti ex (142/217) ×1** — **Flip the Script**: draw 3 if a Pokémon was KO'd last
  turn — a single-prize deck's comeback button. (It's your one two-prize liability, so bench it
  carefully.)
- **Air Balloon (181/217) ×1** — retreat/mobility.
- **Energy (6): 4 Telepathic Psychic Energy + 1 Psychic + 1 Enriching Energy** — Powerful Hand
  only needs one Psychic, so the count is tiny. **Enriching Energy** is the deck's lone ACE SPEC
  (Colorless + a draw). Your Genesect turns off *their* ACE SPECs, not yours.

## Ideal early game
- **Turn 1–2:** **Poffin** down Abra(s), Dunsparce, and your tech Basics; **Dawn/Hilda** to
  find the evolution pieces. Get the draw engine online — evolve into Kadabra/Alakazam and
  **Dudunsparce Run Away Draw** to start churning.
- **Against Dragapult specifically:** get **Elgyem** working early to strip/relocate their
  first manual attachment, slam a **Nighttime Mine** to start the stadium war, and get a **Tool
  onto Genesect** so Unfair Stamp is dead. Now they can't disrupt you and can't reliably attack.

## The core loop
Draw as deep as you safely can (Kadabra/Alakazam evolutions + Dudunsparce, refilled by Dawn/
Hilda/Poké Pad), then **Powerful Hand** for a one-shot. Between swings, **Elgyem** shuffles
their Energy, **Enhanced Hammer (looped with Dedenne)** deletes their Special Energy, **Genesect**
keeps ACE SPECs off the table, and **Nighttime Mine** keeps their Stadium buried. You can play
this as a **race** (just draw-and-KO) or as **full control** (hammer/relocate/deny until they
have nothing) — pick the lane per matchup.

## Priorities / rules of thumb
- **Manage your hand size** — this is the skill of the deck. Versus **Zoroark** and **Judge**,
  a giant hand is a liability. Near ~20 cards or a full bench, **stop drawing** or spend the
  low-value cards (thin out Buddy-Buddy Poffin) so a Judge/Zoroark doesn't blow you out.
- **Count to the KO before you draw more** — you only need enough cards in hand to reach the
  number. Overdrawing past lethal just feeds disruption.
- **Keep a Tool on Genesect** at all times in ACE-SPEC matchups; losing it re-opens Unfair
  Stamp.
- **Loop the Hammers with Dedenne** the moment the opponent leans on Special Energy.
- **Win the Stadium war** — don't be shy about replaying Nighttime Mine to keep the opponent's
  Watchtower/Jamming Tower off the board.

## Weaknesses / bad matchups
- **Cynthia's Garchomp — the real one.** Cerys's *only* loss at Indy (round 5). It's close-to-
  slightly-bad: you must KO **three Garchomp** instead of two Lopunny (an extra Alakazam and a
  whole turn of Dedenne you may not have), it has strong single-prize attackers, Roselia rushes
  you early, and their **Judge + Rocky Fighting Energy + Rocket's Watchtower** end-game combo can
  steal it. Handheld Fan and the Dedenne/Enhanced-Hammer loop are your tools here.
- **Two Zoroark.** One Zoroark is manageable if you don't overdraw; **two** forces so much
  discard that you have to pivot to a pure control win-con. Sacred Ash / Lana's Aid / a Dedenne
  turn buy the cards back.
- **Rocket's Articuno + a Team Rocket's Pokémon** — the scariest hard counter to see.
- **Red Card (Chaos Rising era).** Real disruption, but widely *over*-valued as an "answer" —
  it isn't a hard counter on its own (Alakazam won Champions League Osaka with Red Card legal,
  in part because opponents disrespected it). Two Red Card **plus** Watchtowers starts to get
  there.
- **It is hard to pilot.** This is a high-skill, practice-heavy deck; the control lines reward
  reps. Cerys's own advice: trust your gut on the deck you actually believe in.

## Quick reference — the numbers
- **22 Pokémon** — 4 Abra, 4 Kadabra, 3 Alakazam, 3 Dunsparce, 3 Dudunsparce, 1 Dedenne,
  1 Elgyem, 1 Genesect, 1 Fezandipiti ex, 1 Shaymin.
- **32 Trainers** — 4 Dawn, 3 Hilda, 2 Boss's Orders, 1 Lana's Aid, 4 Buddy-Buddy Poffin,
  4 Poké Pad, 3 Rare Candy, 2 Enhanced Hammer, 1 Sacred Ash, 1 Night Stretcher, 1 Lucky Helmet,
  1 Handheld Fan, 1 Air Balloon, 4 Nighttime Mine.
- **6 Energy** — 4 Telepathic Psychic Energy, 1 Psychic, 1 Enriching Energy (ACE SPEC).
- **Powerful Hand:** 20 damage × cards in hand, for `[P]`. 140-HP, one-prize attacker.
