# How to Pilot — Cynthia's Garchomp (2026)

_Format: Scarlet & Violet-on Standard, the Mega-era Dragapult format. A **single-Stadium,
Fighting-type beatdown** built entirely around the **Cynthia's** tribe — a 400-HP attacker
that hits for 350 and can't be disrupted off its Energy._

## One-line identity
Evolve into **Cynthia's Garchomp ex**, bolt on **+70 HP** and a **can't-be-effected** Energy,
stack **Cynthia's Roserade** on the bench for free damage, and swing for **260–350 with
Draconic Buster**. It's a wall that punches through anything in the format and shrugs off the
disruption that keeps other decks honest. The trade-off: Draconic Buster **discards all your
Energy** every time, so the whole deck is about re-loading faster than the opponent can answer.

## The engine (how the damage happens)
- **Cynthia's Garchomp ex (111/217) ×3** — Stage 2, **330 HP**, Fighting.
  - **Corkscrew Dive** `[F]` — **100** and **draw until you have 6 cards**. Your turn-one-online
    attack: chip for 100 *and* refill your hand while you build the board.
  - **Draconic Buster** `[F][F]` — **260**, then **discard all Energy from this Pokémon**. The
    payoff. Two Energy in, a knockout out — but you start next turn bare, so you must re-attach.
- **Cynthia's Gabite (110/217) ×4** — Stage 1, 100 HP. **Champion's Call**: once per turn,
  **search your deck for any Cynthia's Pokémon to hand**. This is the glue — it finds the next
  Garchomp, a Roserade, a Roselia, whatever the turn needs, every single turn.
- **Cynthia's Gible (109/217) ×4** — Basic, 70 HP. **Rock Hurl** `[F]` 20 (ignores Resistance) —
  a fine turn-1 poke while you set up.
- **Cynthia's Roselia (7/182) ×4 → Cynthia's Roserade (8/182) ×4** — the damage engine.
  **Roserade's "Cheer On to Glory"**: **your Cynthia's Pokémon do +30 to the opponent's Active**
  (before Weakness/Resistance). It **stacks** — two Roserade on the bench is **+60 to every
  attack**. Roselia's **Spike Sting** `[C]` 20 is a free early attacker while the line comes up.

## The damage math (why it one-shots the format)
Draconic Buster is **260** base. Add the boosters:
- **+30 per Cynthia's Roserade** on your bench (Cheer On to Glory, stacks).
- **+30 from Premium Power Pro** (Item, Fighting, this turn only).

So: **260 → 290** (1 Roserade) **→ 320** (2 Roserade) **→ 350** (2 Roserade + Premium Power Pro).
That's enough to **one-shot the 330-HP Mega/ex** attackers the format is built on. Even
**Corkscrew Dive** climbs to 130–160 with Roserade down, so your "draw" attack still trades.

## The wall (why it doesn't die back)
- **Cynthia's Power Weight (162/182) ×3** — Tool: **+70 HP** to the Cynthia's Pokémon it's on.
  Garchomp ex becomes a **400-HP** body — above the one-shot range of most of the field.
- **Rocky Fighting Energy (87/88) ×4** — special Fighting Energy that **prevents all *effects* of
  the opponent's attacks** on the Pokémon it's attached to (damage still lands). Your attacker
  can't be **gusted around by attack effects, Energy-denied, paralyzed, or shut off** — it just
  keeps swinging. This is the piece that makes the deck so frustrating to disrupt.
- Net result: a **400-HP, effect-proof attacker** that hits for 350. To beat it you have to
  out-race it or hit numbers almost nothing in the format can.

## Support / tech (the trainer suite)
- **Fighting Gong (187/217) ×4** — Item: **search for a Basic Fighting Energy _or_ a Basic
  Fighting Pokémon**. This is the re-load valve — after Draconic Buster discards everything,
  Fighting Gong (plus your attachment for turn) puts you back to `[F][F]` fast, and early it
  digs for Gible.
- **Buddy-Buddy Poffin (184/217) ×4** — fetch two ≤70-HP Basics (Gible, Roselia) to explode the
  board turn 1.
- **Premium Power Pro (199/217) ×1** — the +30 damage burst above; save it for the turn you need
  to reach a specific number.
- **Prism Tower (80/86) ×3** — Stadium: **discard 2 to draw 1**, each player's turn. Cheap card
  filtering to dig for Energy/pieces, and — just as important — **three copies win the Stadium
  war**, keeping the opponent's Stadium off the board.
- **Prime Catcher (119/131) ×1** — ACE SPEC gust **+ switch your own Active**; your one big
  reposition to drag up the KO or reset a stuck attacker.
- **Boss's Orders (183/217) ×3** — the every-turn gust to pick off setup Pokémon or take the
  Garchomp KO on your terms.
- **Hilda (84/86) ×1** — Supporter: grab **an Evolution + an Energy** in one card — the perfect
  "I have nothing" recovery that advances the Garchomp line and re-arms it at once.
- **Lillie's Determination (192/217) ×4 · Poké Pad (81/88) ×4 · Pokégear 3.0 (84/86) ×3** — the
  raw-consistency shell: draw, item-draw/search, and Supporter-finding so you hit Gabite every
  turn.
- **Switch (130/132) ×1 · Night Stretcher (196/217) ×1** — mobility and recovery (pull a KO'd
  Garchomp or discarded Energy back).

## Energy (8)
**4 Rocky Fighting Energy + 4 basic Fighting.** Tiny count for a deck that re-attaches constantly,
which is why **Fighting Gong** is really your fifth-through-eighth Energy card. Lead your Rocky
Fighting Energy onto the **attacking** Garchomp (that's where the effect-proofing matters); basic
Fighting is fine on the back-up.

## Ideal early game
- **Turn 1:** **Buddy-Buddy Poffin** down Gible(s) and Roselia; get **Corkscrew Dive** or Rock
  Hurl in for chip while you draw. Start a **Roselia → Roserade** on the bench.
- **Turns 2–3:** evolve **Gible → Gabite → Garchomp ex**, using **Champion's Call** every turn to
  keep the line and the Roserade coming. Attach **Power Weight** + a **Rocky Fighting Energy**,
  land the first **Draconic Buster**. Now you have a 400-HP effect-proof attacker with a boosted
  swing — the game is on your terms.

## The core loop
Each turn: **Champion's Call** for the piece you need → refill Energy (attachment + **Fighting
Gong**) → **Draconic Buster** for 320–350 → the discard leaves you empty, so **re-arm next turn**.
A second Garchomp ex on the bench (also under Power Weight) means a KO on your attacker just
promotes the next wall. Keep **two Roserade** alive on the bench and you're one-shotting the whole
format; **Boss's Orders / Prime Catcher** choose *which* thing dies.

## Priorities / rules of thumb
- **Bench two Roserade early and protect them.** +60 is the difference between a two-shot and a
  one-shot on the format's 330-HP attackers. They're your real "damage cards."
- **Rocky Fighting Energy goes on the attacker.** The effect-proofing only matters where the
  hits are landing — don't waste it on the bench.
- **Respect the discard.** Draconic Buster empties the attacker. Sequence so you have the next
  attachment + a **Fighting Gong** lined up *before* you swing, or you'll whiff a turn.
- **Champion's Call every turn, no exceptions** — a missed Gabite trigger is a missed tempo turn.
- **Win the Stadium war** with Prism Tower; don't let an opposing Stadium sit.
- **You're a two-prize deck** — each Garchomp gives up 2, so the opponent needs three KOs (6
  prizes). Don't bench extra ex-Pokémon you don't need; keep the prize map in your favour.

## Matchups
- **Great into the one-prize decks (Alakazam, Lucario, Raging Bolt, Hydreigon).** They trade
  little single-prize Pokémon and can't profitably answer a 400-HP effect-proof wall; you out-size
  them and Boss around their engine. This is exactly why Cynthia's Garchomp handed the
  Indianapolis-winning Alakazam its **only loss** — Alakazam has to KO **three Garchomp** (an
  extra attacker and a whole extra turn) while Roselia rushes early and Rocky Fighting shrugs off
  the Enhanced-Hammer/Elgyem disruption.
- **Dragapult.** A real race — Phantom Dive spreads onto your bench (Roselia/Gabite) and can two-
  shot your board, and it's a Stage-2 mirror of setup speed. Power Weight walls single Phantom
  Dives; you need Roserade up fast and to gust the Dreepy/Drakloak line before it's online.
- **Anything that races on damage output** is the danger — you're powerful but a touch slow to
  re-arm, so decks that ignore your wall and just out-prize the board pressure you.

## Weaknesses / bad matchups
- **The Energy discard is the whole risk.** Every Draconic Buster resets you to zero; a turn where
  you can't re-attach `[F][F]` is a turn you don't knock out, and good opponents will make you
  choose. Fighting Gong and disciplined sequencing are the fix.
- **Ability denial** hurts — turning off Champion's Call (search) or Cheer On to Glory (damage)
  cripples the plan more than removing Energy does.
- **Being out-prized by a faster deck** — you give up 2 per Garchomp, so a deck that races
  single-prize attackers past your re-load can get to 6 before you stabilize.
- **High skill ceiling on the re-load math.** The deck is easy to swing but hard to *sequence*:
  counting to lethal, banking the next attachment, and keeping two Roserade alive under spread is
  the real skill.
